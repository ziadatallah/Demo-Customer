1. What ISAF does

ISAF generates the plans, standards, checklists, records and reports your program needs for certification (DO-178C, DO-254, ARP4754B and related standards) from one configuration workbook. You describe your program once; ISAF fills every document with your company, program, people, items, levels and dates, phase by phase.

Unlock→Program Intake→Config Data→Save workbook→Build→Manage documents

Using the web app hosted by MX Aero?Open the Web App Guide instead; the web app's Help button opens it automatically.

Your app is licensed to your company. Some values (company, program type, authority, certification basis and others) come from your licence and are locked. Only the program phases in your licence can be built.

ISAF is not a qualified toolISAF is a document generation aid. It is not a qualified tool under DO-178C section 12.2.1 or DO-330, and no certification credit comes from using it. DO-178C requires a tool to be qualified only where its output is not verified; ISAF's output is verified, because you review and approve every document before it becomes life cycle data. That is what keeps ISAF outside the qualification requirement, so the review is not a formality. The cover of every document ISAF generates carries this notice, so a DER or an authority reviewing your data sees it too. ISAF does generate the DO-330 qualification documents (TQP, TOR, TVCP, TVR, TQAS) for your own tools; that is a different thing from ISAF being qualified.

2. Open the app and enter your access key

  1. Installed app: open the ISAF folder and double-click START.command (Mac) or START.bat (Windows). Keep the window that opens running while you work. Your browser opens the app (normally http://localhost:3000).
    Hosted trial: open the web address you were given.
  2. The app is locked until you enter your company's access key. Type it in the Access key box and select Unlock.
  3. When the key is accepted, the yellow box disappears and the app opens with your starter configuration loaded. A wrong key shows "That access key was not accepted. Check it and try again."; check the key with your ISAF administrator.

4. Your starter configuration and licensed fields

When the app opens, it loads a starter configuration prepared from your licence. It already contains your company name and address, program type, primary authority, certification regime and basis, DO-160 scope and revision, and your requirements tool type.

  • Licensed fields show (licensed) next to their name and are greyed out. They cannot be changed in the app, and editing them in Excel has no effect: the app restores them whenever it loads, saves or builds a workbook.
  • If a licensed value is wrong (for example, your address has changed), contact MX Aero. MX Aero corrects your licence and sends an updated app; your saved workbooks keep all your other entries.
  • Your job is to complete everything else, then save it as your own workbook (section 6). From then on, open your own workbook, not the starter.

5. Program Intake, step by step

Program Intake guides you through the program-level questions in three steps. Required fields that are still empty are outlined in red; the red disappears as soon as you fill them. The line under the step tabs counts how many required fields are complete.

Step 1 — Program

  1. Select the Program Intake tab. The starter configuration is already open; you do not need Start New Intake unless you want a blank program.
  2. In Company And Program, enter the Company Short Name, Program Name, Program Short Name, the Document Revision and, optionally, CAGE code, document prefix, intake owner and a short product description.
  3. In Branding And Document Control, choose brand colours, select Browse… to pick your company logo (SVG, PNG or JPEG), select Choose ECCN to set the export control marking, and enter the document retention period. The distribution statement starts as "© Copyright - All rights reserved"; change it if your company uses another.
  4. Select Continue. Each step opens at the top of the page.

Step 2 — Approval & Scope

  1. Choose the Approval Path (for example, FAA TSO authorization). Only paths that fit your program type and licence can be chosen; others are marked "(not for LRU programs)" or "(not in your licence)".
  2. Complete the certification basis: Certification Regulations / Basis, Applicable TSO / ETSO (if no TSO applies, enter Not applicable: followed by the reason), any secondary authority and the authority project reference.
  3. Answer the scope questions: software, hardware, external suppliers and DO-160 in scope; the aircraft and system FHA classifications and failure-condition counts; and whether CCA, ZSA, PRA and DO-254 Appendix B apply.
  4. Enter your primary authority contact and any partnering arrangement, means-of-compliance issues or other issues.
  5. Select Continue.

Step 3 — People & Dates

  1. For each of the ten roles (Chief Engineer, Certification Lead, Program Manager, Systems, Safety, Software, Hardware, Process Assurance, Quality and Configuration Manager), enter a name and an email. One person may hold several roles: enter their name and email for each role.
  2. Choose the Independence Mechanism (how process and quality assurance independence is achieved) and describe it.
  3. Enter the target milestone dates, from CoDR to entry into service. If a milestone does not apply, enter Not applicable: and the reason.
  4. Select Continue to Config Data.
Not sure yet? Use TBDSelect the TBD button under a field to mark it "to be determined". You can save and come back later; search for TBD in Config Data to find them. A required field must be resolved before building.

6. Save your configuration workbook

Your answers live in the browser until you save them. Save often.

  1. Select Save Configuration Workbook (in Program Intake or Config Data).
  2. Installed app: choose a folder and file name. Hosted trial: your browser asks where to save, or downloads the file.
  3. The saved copy is loaded straight away and becomes the workbook used by Build. Each save creates a new version of the file, so earlier versions are kept.
  4. Next time, open your own workbook from the Build tab (section 9, step 2) or with Load Existing Config in Program Intake.
Keep your workbook safeThe workbook is your program's master data. Store it where your company keeps program records (and in your configuration management system). In a hosted trial, nothing is kept on the server.

8. Fix problems before you build

ISAF checks your configuration with the same rules the build uses, while you work, so you see problems before you press Build.

  1. Look at the Config Data sub-tabs. A red tab with a number has problems that block the build; an amber tab has warnings only.
  2. Open a red tab. The panel at the top lists each problem in plain words, with a hint where it helps.
  3. Select a problem. ISAF takes you to the field, highlights it and puts the cursor in it. Fix it; the list updates within a second.
  4. Repeat until no tab is red. Each tab then shows "✓ No problems found on this tab". Save the workbook.
WarningsWarnings (amber) do not stop the build. They point to something worth reviewing, for example DO-160 in scope while no hardware item is marked as needing DO-160 testing.

9. Build your documents

  1. Select the Build tab.
  2. Choose the workbook to build from: your loaded workbook is shown in the green box. To open another, drag the .xlsx file onto the box or select it and choose the file. Your starter configuration reloads the starter.
  3. Leave Build source on Standard build (see the i button for New template drafts).
  4. Under Program phases, tick the milestone(s) to build, for example SOI 1. Each phase generates the documents first needed at that gate. All selects every licensed phase. Phases outside your licence are greyed out.
  5. Installed app: check the Output folder; select Browse… to change it and Open Folder to view it. Optionally choose a Program logo for this build.
  6. Look under the Build button. "✓ Configuration checks passed" means you can build. If it lists problems, select a link to go to the field (section 8).
  7. Select Build. A progress bar and timer show the build running; Stop build cancels it.
  8. When the build finishes, the Build log shows how many documents were generated and any failures. Installed app: the documents are in the output folder and in Program Documents. Hosted trial: download the ZIP when it is offered; it is removed from the server shortly afterwards.
Build again after changesAfter changing the configuration, save the workbook and build again. In documents your team has edited and checked in to Program Documents, a standard build updates the configuration values you did not change and keeps your text (see "Reuse your edited documents" below). Under document control the updated version is saved as a candidate for review.

Reuse your edited documents

Use this when you have edited generated documents in Word and then change the configuration, or when you start a new program from a previous program's documents.

  1. On the Build tab, under Reuse edited documents, select Choose files… to pick Word documents, or Choose folder… to pick a whole folder, for example the output folder of your last build or a previous program's documents. Only .docx files are used; select either button again to add more, or Clear to start over.
  2. Build as usual. ISAF matches each document to the document it builds: by document number for the same program, or by document type and item for a new program.
  3. In each matched document, the configuration values you did not change are updated: names, part numbers, people, dates and references to other documents. Your own text, tables and formatting are kept.
  4. Where you changed a value and the configuration value has also changed, your text is kept and a Word comment by "ISAF refresh" marks it. Review it, update it if needed and delete the comment.
  5. Open Refresh_Report.txt in the output. It lists each refreshed document, every flagged value and any file that was not used.
Good to knowThis works with documents generated by ISAF 5.3.26 or later. Documents checked in to Program Documents are refreshed automatically, so you need this only for files kept outside the app. New content that has no place in your edited document, such as sections for a newly added item, is not inserted; the report lists it, and New template drafts gives you the complete new document to compare.

Work in a folder instead of ZIP files

In Chrome or Edge (and the ISAF desktop app) you can choose a folder on your computer for the program's documents. ISAF then reads your edited documents from it and saves each build into it, so you no longer collect documents from ZIP files after every build.

  1. On the Build tab, under Working folder, select Choose working folder… (or Build → Working Folder…), pick or create a folder, and allow ISAF to view and save files there when the browser asks.
  2. Keep Read edited documents from this folder and save the build into it selected and build as usual. The documents you edited in the folder are reused (see "Reuse your edited documents"), and the new documents are saved into the folder in one subfolder per phase (for example SOI-2).
  3. Before ISAF replaces a file, it copies the previous version to _ISAF_backup in the folder, in a subfolder named with the date and time. A document saved under a new name (for example a new revision) moves the older file there too, so the folder holds one current file per document.
  4. The browser remembers the folder. It may ask again for permission the next time you build. Select Stop using this folder to go back to the usual output.
Good to knowClose documents in Word before building or updating; a file that is open may not be replaced, and ISAF lists it. Other browsers (Safari, Firefox) cannot save into a folder; there ISAF keeps the ZIP download.

Import and review requirements

The Import tab checks your requirements before the human review. It eases the manual work of a requirements review; it is not a qualified tool, and the reviewer still performs, completes and signs the review.

  1. Export your requirements from your requirements tool as ReqIF (.reqif or .reqifz, from DOORS, DOORS Next, Polarion, Jama and others), or keep them in Excel (.xlsx) or CSV (any layout: see Connecting your requirements tool for the columns and an example file): system requirements, software high-level and low-level requirements, hardware requirements and verification cases (tests).
  2. On the Import tab (or Documents → Import Requirements…), select Add requirements files…. Each file appears as a card.
  3. Jama Connect and Polarion users can skip the export (see Connecting your requirements tool for everything your tool administrator has to provide): open Read requirements straight from Jama Connect or …from Polarion, enter the service address and the credentials your tool administrator creates (an API client ID and secret for Jama, a personal access token for Polarion; both are shown once), then Test connection, Load projects, tick the item or work item types that hold requirements or verification cases, and Read requirements. Each type becomes a card, with the tool’s own field names as its columns and its links already in place as trace links. ISAF only reads: nothing in your requirements tool is changed. The connection is kept on your own ISAF server and the secret is never shown again.
  4. For each file, check the Level and the columns ISAF has chosen: ID and Requirement text are needed; Traces to (parent IDs), Derived, Rationale and Verification method make the review more complete. For system and hardware requirements, also map Validation method, Validation conclusion, Assumption, Safety-related, DAL and (system) Source of requirement when your export has them; checks that need a column you do not map are skipped or noted once. A ReqIF file with several specifications (modules) gets a level for each. The example line under the card shows how the first requirement is read. Your column choices are remembered in this browser.
  5. Open Review options to match your requirements standard: the list of ambiguous or open terms, the maximum length, and which rules apply.
  6. Select Run review. The results show, per level, how many requirements trace to a parent, are developed further and have tests; the review checklist evidence; and every finding (Error, Warning or Info) with the requirement, its text and the reference of its standard: DO-178C for software, DO-254 for hardware (for example tolerances, validation of derived requirements, safety requirements with a DAL), ARP4754A and ARP4761A for system requirements (for example source identified, tolerances, validation method and conclusion, assumptions, safety requirements allocated with a DAL). The checklist evidence is grouped the same way. Filter by severity, level or text.
  7. Select Download error report (Excel) for the workbook: Summary, Findings (with Disposition and Reviewer notes columns for the review), Requirements, Trace and Checklist evidence.

Using the requirements in your documents. After the review, select Use these requirements in every build. From then on a build writes them into the documents as it generates them — there is nothing to do afterwards. The Build tab says what will be written in before the build starts, and the build log and Requirements_Report.txt say what was:

  • HLR, LLR, hardware requirements (HRD) and system requirements (REQ-Sys): a requirements table (ID, text, parent, derived, verification method, number of findings) and a table of derived requirements with their rationale and a column for the safety assessment feedback (REQ-Sys: the downward trace).
  • Trace: the trace matrix (traces to, developed by, verified by) and a gap register with a Disposition column.
  • SVCP: the imported verification cases and the requirements each verifies.
  • VMAT-Sys (system validation matrix): the validation matrix (source, DAL, validation method, conclusion; associated function and evidence references for you to complete), the status totals and the open validation findings.
  • PR-SWRD, PR-SDD, PR-HRD and Sys-SRR review checklists: evidence in the Finding / Notes column of each criterion the data can speak to (for example "2 of 3 software high-level requirements trace to a system requirement; not traced: HLR-3"), and a reminder that judgment criteria are for the reviewer. Pass / Fail / N/A is never ticked.
  1. Check each file's Item: the software or hardware item its requirements belong to (filled in when the program has only one). A ReqIF file with several specifications has an item for each.
  2. Select Use these requirements in every build, then build. A build writes the requirements attached when it ran; ISAF does not re-read your requirements tool during a build.
  3. Import again whenever the requirements change and build again: ISAF replaces the tables and evidence it inserted (marked "ISAF import" in Word) and keeps everything else, including the reviewer's notes next to the evidence. If someone edited an inserted table in Word, it is still replaced, and a Word comment says so; change requirements in the requirements tool.
  4. Documents that were already generated (ISAF Admin only). Use this block when you want to change only the requirements tables in documents you already have: a build regenerates the whole document from the current configuration, while this edits the blocks marked "ISAF import" and leaves the rest exactly as it was issued. With a working folder (see "Work in a folder instead of ZIP files") it is one click: ISAF rewrites the documents in that folder in place, keeps the previous copies in _ISAF_backup and writes Import_Report.txt beside them. Without one — in a browser that cannot write into a folder — the updated documents come back as a ZIP for you to unpack and put back yourself, and where a rebuild is possible it is less work. The block says which of the two you will get.
Good to knowChecklist questions that can be answered from the data (identification, wording, units, verification method, trace) show "No findings" or "Findings" with evidence; questions that need engineering judgment (accuracy, consistency, compatibility with the target, algorithms) are marked "Reviewer". Your files are read and deleted at once; nothing is kept in Program Documents.

10. Program Focal and the program register

In the installed app, each program has a register that records every generated or imported document, its versions, check-outs, releases and baselines.

  1. In the program bar, type your name in Program Focal. Every action you record (build, check-in, release) is labelled with this name. Each person uses their own name.
  2. With your workbook loaded, select Create program. Check the company, program name and document prefix, then select Create program. (If you skip this, the first build creates the register automatically.)
  3. From then on, choose the program in Repository program when you work on it.

11. Program Documents and document control

Program Documents (installed app) lists every document in the program register with its document PN, title, owner, milestone, working version, released version and check-out status.

  1. Use Search documents and the category filter to find a document. Select its document PN to open its details.
  2. To edit a document: in its details, choose Check out and download, edit the file in Word, then use Check in edited file with a short change summary. While it is checked out, others cannot check it in.
  3. Select Import document to add a document you already have (for example a supplier plan).
  4. When your program is ready for formal control, select Enable document control. From then on, builds use checked-in documents, and releases and reviews are recorded.
  5. At a milestone, select Establish baseline to freeze the exact set of released documents; Download exact set retrieves it later.
  6. Open Certification preparation (top of the tab) to work through the checklist of records to review before an authority submission.
Your company's procedureThe Document_Configuration_Control.docx procedure supplied with the app explains authoring, review, release and baseline responsibilities in detail. Adopt or adapt it as your company procedure.

12. Document PNs

Document PNs defines how document part numbers are formed: prefix, discipline (SYS/SW/HW), document type code, sequence length and starting number, separator and revision format.

  1. Open Document PNs. The scheme stored in your workbook is shown.
  2. Adjust the settings and the document type codes your company uses. The Live examples show the resulting numbers.
  3. Select Apply scheme to configuration, then Save Configuration Workbook. Once numbers are issued, the register keeps them across builds.

13. Roadmap and Administration

Roadmap shows document actions across the program: owners, target dates and dependencies, with filters for documents waiting on others, overdue, or assigned to you. Owners and dates are set in each document's register entry.

Administration shows the app edition, hosting mode, recent build activity and service status. Most users do not need it.

14. Working over time: changes, new versions, backups

  • Change your configuration: open your latest workbook, change it in Program Intake or Config Data, save (a new version is created) and build again.
  • Unsaved changes: if you edit and then select Build, ISAF asks you to save first, so the documents always match a saved workbook.
  • New ISAF version: the app tells you when a newer version is available, with a bar across the top of the window saying what is new and where to get it. ISAF never installs anything by itself: your IT installs the new app, you open it, enter your key and open your latest workbook; your entries carry over and licensed values are updated automatically. You can also ask at any time with Help → Check for Updates.
    The bar saysWhat it means
    Update available (blue)Install it when it suits you. Remind me later hides it for a week, Skip this version until the next release. Each person chooses for themself.
    Critical update (red)The release affects a major function, so it must be installed. The bar cannot be dismissed and shows the days left. When that period ends the app stops building documents and stops writing imported requirements into documents until it is updated; reading your configuration, Config Data and Program Documents keep working.
    Pass a critical update to whoever installs ISAF on your server as soon as you see it. If you cannot install it in time, contact MX Aero at the address shown on the bar.
  • Backups (installed app): back up your configuration workbooks, the output folder and the program register (the 07_Web_Service/repository folder inside the ISAF folder, unless your administrator moved it).
  • Hosted trial: download every build and keep your workbook. Nothing is kept on the server.
